Pine Point Medical: should a primary care clinic sell IV drips?

Role strategy consultant, financial model ownerTeam twoOutcome $1.3M profit opportunity; strategy adopted

the question

Pine Point Medical is a well-established primary care practice in Ithaca. They'd recently expanded capacity and wanted to use it to diversify revenue. Consumer IV therapy, the wellness drips that celebrities made popular, was the idea on the table. Leadership wanted to know whether it made sense for a real medical practice to get into it, and if so, how.

what we did

We sized the market from the top down, then built a bottom-up financial model from the clinic's actual constraints: rooms, staff time, supply costs, and the patient segments most likely to say yes. We benchmarked the local competitive set to understand pricing and positioning, and mapped the cross-sell path from the practice's existing aesthetics clients, who were the most natural first customers.

The clinic's advantage wasn't the drip. It was that a doctor's office selling wellness services is more credible than a strip-mall IV bar, and the model needed to price that credibility in.

what we found

A $1.3M annual profit opportunity at an 86.7% margin, using a fraction of the new capacity during weekday hours. We recommended launching as a pilot with a base offering and optional add-ons, so the practice could learn what patients wanted before committing to a full menu, and then formalize a tiered model that also simplified bulk ordering. The clinic adopted the strategy.

the second project

The same summer I looked at a different opportunity for them: DEXA body-composition scans. By benchmarking more than ten regional providers and building a pricing strategy and commercialization plan, we identified about $200K in annual revenue, and the plan drove a 20% increase in bookings.

what i learned

Healthcare taught me that a good model has to respect the operator's reality. The spreadsheet said four rooms; the practice manager knew which four and why. Both of us were right, and the recommendation was better for including both.
