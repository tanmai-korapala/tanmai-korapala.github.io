Ithaca Health Alliance · Johnson Board Fellow · August 2024 to May 2025

Ithaca Health Alliance: what the board didn't know about its donors.

Role donor strategy and revenue analysis for the BoardData five years of donor recordsOutcome risk quantified; guidance adopted into long-term planning

the setup

Ithaca Health Alliance is a nonprofit that provides free health care to people who can't otherwise get it. As a Johnson Board Fellow, I sat with their Board of Directors for a year, and my project was a full analysis of the organization's fundraising base: more than $500K in annual donations across 370+ donors.

what i looked at

Donor base composition and growth over five years. Revenue concentration by segment. Retention and lapse rates overall, for first-time donors, and for major donors. Reactivation opportunities on two- and five-year lookbacks. And campaign-by-campaign performance, including seasonality. The goal was to move the board from "how much did we raise" to "how healthy is the machine that raises it."

The organization had just had its best fundraising year in five. The analysis showed that the same year had also quietly increased its dependence on a small number of sources.

what i found

A revenue concentration risk the organization hadn't previously quantified: too much of the total came from a few segments that fluctuate year to year. I modeled retention and reactivation scenarios to show what a modest improvement in donor retention would be worth, and delivered the findings as structured guidance directly to the board, who carried it into their long-term growth planning.

what i learned

Presenting to a board of directors is different from presenting to a client. They're volunteers, they care deeply, and they have twenty minutes. I learned to lead with the one number that changes a decision and keep the rest in the appendix.
