Xbox Game Trials: try before you buy.

Role user research, monetization, go-to-marketTeam sixFormat feature proposal with working prototype

the problem

A new AAA game costs $70 to $80 in 2026, and 62% of gamers say they no longer buy full-price games. Demo discs are gone, rental stores are gone, in-store kiosks are gone, and few developers build demos. Buying a game has become a high-stakes gamble, and our interviews kept turning up the same regret: it looked great on YouTube and was boring to play.

the feature

Game Trials: click "Try" and you're playing the full game in seconds through Xbox Cloud Gaming, for a trial window the developer sets. Progress carries over if you buy, and you can buy mid-trial with one click. Built entirely on infrastructure Xbox already has, which is what made it high value and high feasibility at the same time.

Three users, three different coping strategies, one problem: no amount of research replaces actually playing the game.

what we did

Interviewed players across risk-avoiders, over-researchers, and price-sensitive buyers, surveyed 50+ active gamers (the majority rated purchase anxiety as very or extremely significant, and two hours was the preferred trial length), built a clickable prototype, and evaluated three monetization models: free for all, subscription-gated, and hybrid. Then a customer acquisition and promotional plan tied to the next-generation console launch.
