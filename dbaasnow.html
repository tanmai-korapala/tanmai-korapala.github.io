DBaasNow: why a $68.6M market had zero customers.

Role growth and strategy, reporting to the C-suiteTeam cross-functional, five countriesOutcome 5-year plan adopted; 500+ leads

the situation

DBaasNow is an early-stage B2B database-as-a-service platform. When I joined, the company had a real product, a market that sized to $68.6M, and no customers. Everyone had a theory about why. Nobody had tested one.

the diagnosis

I mapped the adoption barriers across nine competitors to understand what actually stopped a buyer from switching database providers. The answer wasn't price or features. It was that the buyers with the highest willingness to pay were the ones with the most to lose from a migration, and nothing in our pitch addressed that fear. That reframing became a five-year plan, prioritizing verticals by willingness to pay and sequencing how we'd earn trust in each one. The C-suite adopted it, and it's still the structure they use to run pitch and demo calls.

The company didn't have a product problem or a marketing problem. It had a trust problem, and once you name that, the strategy writes itself.

building the machine

Strategy is only useful if someone runs it, so I built the pieces. The company's first marketing strategy, with an intern I managed and a steady stream of content experiments to learn what landed with target buyers: impressions grew 118% and reactions 162% in six weeks. A CRM built from scratch, with outreach sequences I designed and tested, which generated 500+ leads. And a redesigned customer acquisition workflow, after pinpointing where prospects dropped out of the funnel, executed with a team spread across five countries. Product engagement grew more than 150%.

what i learned

Startups are where you find out whether you can do the whole loop: diagnose, decide, build, measure, adjust. I'd done pieces of that at GM inside a large organization. Here I had to do all of it with almost no resources, which turned out to be the best strategy training I've had.
